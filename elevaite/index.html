01 / The challenge

One camera. Three safety-critical events.

Residential elevator cameras generate continuous footage, creating an opportunity for AI-assisted monitoring to identify safety-critical events and support timely human response. ElevAIte explores how computer vision and temporal modeling can identify distinct risks while keeping the final alert understandable to building operators.

Design objective

Build a unified pipeline that processes elevator video, validates behavior across time, combines three specialized detection modules, and communicates why an alert was raised.

Why it is difficult

Elevator footage includes occlusion, changing viewpoints, short events, similar-looking actions, and limited safety-event data. The system therefore needed careful data quality work and temporal—not only frame-level—reasoning.

03 / Dataset & approach

A modular vision pipeline built around time.

The research combined original elevator recordings with public fall, elevator-event, and child/adult datasets. After cleaning and quality review, videos were split at video level to reduce leakage between training and evaluation.

MODULE 01

Fainting detection

MediaPipe Pose extracts 33 body landmarks. Coordinates are organized into 15-frame sequences, then a CNN–BiLSTM learns spatial posture and temporal movement patterns.

MODULE 02

Child-left-alone detection

YOLOv11 detects and tracks children and adults. Temporal presence features and a Logistic Regression classifier determine whether a child remains without an adult.

MODULE 03

Door behavior

Door-state detections are combined with optical-flow motion features so the system can reason about abnormal opening, closing, and movement patterns over time.

Elevator video→Three detection modules→Temporal validation→Priority logic→Explainable alert

04 / System integration

From separate models to one decision.

The modules run as a coordinated monitoring system. A central decision layer validates confidence over time and applies the safety priority faint → child alone → door fault. The interface then presents the detected event with supporting explanation rather than a label alone.

Explainability

Visual and textual cues make alerts easier to inspect, supporting trust and faster operator response.

Engineering focus

Integration required aligning model inputs, outputs, event timing, priority rules, and interface behavior—followed by system-level debugging.

07 / Reflection

What the project demonstrated—and what comes next.

Key learning

  • Building a reliable AI system requires more than accurate models. A robust decision pipeline and temporal reasoning are just as important for consistent real-world performance.
  • Data quality had a greater impact than I expected. Careful data collection, annotation, and validation often improved the system more than changing model architectures.
  • Developing an end-to-end AI solution taught me that successful AI goes beyond model training. It requires thoughtful system design, explainability, and a focus on creating solutions that people can trust.

Limitations & next steps

  • Expand dataset size and demographic/environmental diversity.
  • Improve robustness under pose occlusion and difficult camera angles.
  • Optimize inference for real-time elevator hardware.
  • Evaluate additional safety events and deeper explanation methods.
