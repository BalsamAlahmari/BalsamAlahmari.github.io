01 / The problem

Resume review is slow, inconsistent, and bilingual.

Beamdata's HR team reviews resumes by hand to pull out candidate information. The work is slow and varies from reviewer to reviewer, especially across Arabic and English submissions. Most existing models and benchmarks focus on English resumes, with little evidence of how they behave on Arabic.

What the project delivers

A bilingual evaluation dataset built from real resumes, a benchmark of three models on extraction quality, an infrastructure benchmark of the selected model, and a working web app for resume upload, extraction, and job matching.

Scope

Evaluation only: models were used as-is, with no fine-tuning. Resumes are supported as .docx files. Job matching scores each uploaded resume against a job description that HR provides.

02 / My role

Model quality benchmarking.

I designed and ran the quality evaluation that decided which model went into production.

What I did

  • Compared GPT-5.6 sol, Qwen2.5-14B, and Qwen2.5-7B on the full 72-resume bilingual dataset.
  • Scored every output against human-annotated ground-truth JSON.
  • Measured seven quality dimensions in both languages.
  • Recommended the model with the most consistent and trustworthy behavior, with low hallucination weighted heavily.

The evaluation dataset

  • 72 resumes: 36 English and 36 Arabic.
  • Each paired with a manually annotated ground-truth candidate profile.
  • Every entry programmatically checked against its source .docx and ground-truth file, with no missing or extra entries.

03 / Methodology

Seven dimensions, measured the same way in both languages.

All automated metrics compare each model's structured JSON output against the ground truth and, where needed, the original resume text.

Correctness and completeness

Extraction precision and recall, combined into F1. Values are normalized first (Unicode, case, punctuation, dates), then matched by exact, fuzzy (≥ 0.90 similarity), or token containment. List fields are aligned one-to-one so item order doesn't matter.

Hallucination

Any extra value the model produced is checked against the original resume text, not just the annotation, and classed as source-supported, ambiguous, or hallucinated. This separates real inventions from details the annotator left out.

Relevance and instruction following

Relevance combines schema relevance with how well free text is grounded in the candidate's own resume. Instruction following checks clean JSON output, schema key discipline, and strict date formats.

Structured output

Valid JSON rate, and schema compliance: how many of the expected fields are present in the output, independent of whether their values are right.

Arabic quality

Arabic was not scored with a separate metric. The identical pipeline ran on the Arabic set, with Arabic-specific normalization: unifying equivalent letter forms, mapping Arabic punctuation, treating "present" phrasings as one value, and filtering Arabic stopwords. The scoring logic and thresholds stayed the same for both languages.

05 / The decision

Why Qwen2.5-14B.

It did not win every metric. It was the most consistent and trustworthy model across both languages.

Hallucination decided it

On Arabic, Qwen2.5-14B was the only model with zero hallucinations. Qwen2.5-7B invented information in nearly half of its flagged extras (48.84%), and GPT-5.6 sol reached 6.00%. For HR, inventing a candidate's skills or certifications is unacceptable.

Best balance in English

GPT-5.6 sol had the highest recall but the lowest precision (76.71%), so many of its extracted fields were wrong. Qwen2.5-14B had the highest English F1 (90.52%) and the lowest hallucination rate (0.89%).

06 / Serving the model

Qwen2.5-14B on vLLM, benchmarked under load.

FastAPI reads the text with python-docx, then prompts Qwen2.5-14B on vLLM for a JSON candidate profile.

With a job description added, each resume is scored on skills, experience, education, languages, and role alignment.

08 / What I learned

Test on your own data.

General benchmarks don't predict your task

GPT-5.6 sol had the strongest general reputation, yet Qwen2.5-14B beat it on our extraction task on precision, F1, and hallucination. We would not have known without testing on our own data.

Ground truth is expensive

Writing and checking each annotation by hand against its resume was slow and tiring. It showed why a reliable ground truth is worth building once, so that evaluation can then run automatically.
